FIFO is the rule; rotation is the system that runs it. Rotation adds the tracking, the reminders, and the cleanouts that turn "oldest in front" into a pantry that never lets food expire. If you already do FIFO, this is the upgrade that makes it automatic.

## The four parts of rotation

1. **FIFO placement** — oldest in front, newest in back, every time you shop.
2. **Dated labels** — every opened container carries a name and a date.
3. **Use-first shelf** — a front-and-center shelf for anything approaching its date.
4. **Regular checks** — a weekly scan and a seasonal cleanout.

Each part is small. Together they mean food never sits long enough to expire.

## Setting up rotation

Start where FIFO left off: zones in place, newest behind oldest, labels on containers. Then add the two rotation-specific pieces — the use-first shelf and the check routine.

The use-first shelf lives at eye level in the front. Anything within its final weeks goes there: the can whose date is three months out, the opened cereal you keep skipping, the boxed kit you bought on sale. When you reach for a meal, you reach for that shelf first.

## The weekly rotation check

Once a week, spend 10 minutes scanning the pantry:

- Pull any older items that have drifted to the back up to the front.
- Move anything near its date onto the use-first shelf.
- Spot-check the front rows for dates and open packages.
- Note what's low and what needs using up this week.

That last point is the connection to cooking: the weekly check feeds a "use this up" plan, which is what actually rotates the food — out of the pantry and into meals.

::tip
Turn the weekly check into a shopping rule: before you buy anything new, glance at the use-first shelf. If you already own a similar item, it goes on the "cook this week" list, not in the cart. This single habit halves duplicate buying.
::

## The seasonal cleanout

Four times a year, run the full reset: pull everything out, check every date, consolidate duplicates, and donate or toss anything you won't use. The [seasonal pantry cleanout](/pantry-organization/seasonal-pantry-cleanout/) has the complete checklist. Seasonal cleanouts catch what weekly checks miss and reset the system when it starts to drift.

## Tying it together

Rotation works best with the other pantry systems: [zones](/pantry-organization/pantry-zones-and-layout/) tell you where things live, [labels](/pantry-organization/how-to-label-pantry-containers/) tell you how old they are, and the [inventory checklist](/pantry-organization/pantry-inventory-checklist/) tells you what you have. Rotation is the engine that keeps them all moving.
