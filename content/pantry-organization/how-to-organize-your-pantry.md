A pantry stays organized when it has a system, not when you have willpower. The system that works is simple and mechanical: zones give everything a home, FIFO makes the oldest food get used first, and labels turn mystery containers into tools. Do the reset once, and maintenance drops to minutes a week.

## Step 1 — Empty everything

Pull every item out of the pantry and put it on the counter or a cleared table. This is non-negotiable: you cannot organize what you cannot see. The mess on the counter is the pantry's inventory, and it's the first time in months you'll see all of it at once.

## Step 2 — Sort by zone

Group the food as you pull it out: canned goods, grains and pasta, baking, breakfast, snacks, oils and condiments, spices. Sorting happens naturally during the empty — make piles or use boxes. This is also the moment to spot duplicates: two bags of brown sugar, three jars of marinara.

## Step 3 — Check dates and toss

As each item goes into its pile, check the date and condition. Toss anything expired, anything with pantry bugs, and any damaged packaging. This is where FIFO pays off — the pile of past-date food is the proof that the old system failed. See [how long canned food lasts](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-canned-food-last/) if you're unsure whether a can is safe.

## Step 4 — Clean the shelves

Wipe down the shelves with warm soapy water and let them dry completely. Pantry dust and crumbs attract bugs. While they're drying, decide your zones and where each one lives — the goal is that a stranger could find the peanut butter in ten seconds.

::tip
Donate unopened, in-date food you know you won't eat to a local food bank during the sort. It keeps good food out of the trash and out of next year's reset.
::

## Step 5 — Return by zone, oldest in front

Put the food back by zone, with the newest items in the back and the oldest in front — the FIFO rule. High-use items at eye level, heavy cans on lower shelves, light snacks on top. Leave a little breathing room so nothing gets buried, and put a shelf liner down if you want to protect the shelves.

## Step 6 — Containerize and label

Move opened staples — rice, flour, pasta, cereal — into airtight containers and label each with the food name and the date you opened or filled it. For the label, a date is the difference between "flour" and "flour from 2023." See [how to label pantry containers](/pantry-organization/how-to-label-pantry-containers/) for the label system.

## Step 7 — Maintain with three habits

The reset is a one-time 2–4 hours. The system is three habits you can do with your eyes closed: return food to its zone, put new behind old, and label what you open. Add a 10-minute weekly tidy and the [seasonal pantry cleanout](/pantry-organization/seasonal-pantry-cleanout/), and the pantry stays organized permanently.

Now add the [FIFO system](/pantry-organization/the-fifo-pantry-system/) and set up [pantry zones](/pantry-organization/pantry-zones-and-layout/) to complete the foundation.
