A pantry inventory is the anti-waste layer on top of FIFO. FIFO makes sure the oldest food gets used; the inventory makes sure you actually know what you have — so you stop buying the second bag of rice, and you catch the near-date can before it expires.

## The checklist

Print or copy this and keep it on the pantry door. Update it when you shop and when you cook.

- [ ] **Canned goods** — list items, quantity, and dates
- [ ] **Grains & pasta** — rice, pasta, oats, dried beans, cereal
- [ ] **Baking** — flour, sugar, leaveners, chocolate, extracts
- [ ] **Breakfast** — cereal, granola, bars, pancake mix
- [ ] **Snacks** — chips, crackers, nuts, dried fruit
- [ ] **Oils & condiments** — bottles, vinegars, jarred items
- [ ] **Spices** — note what's low or old
- [ ] **Use soon** — anything approaching its date
- [ ] **Non-food supplies** — bags, foil, paper goods

## How to fill it in

Work zone by zone, top shelf to bottom, so you can't miss anything. For each staple, write one line: item, quantity, and the oldest date you have. Don't catalog every jar — track what you tend to forget or run out of. That's the list's purpose: it's a tool against the specific failure of buying food you already own.

The "use soon" line is where the inventory meets rotation. Anything within its final weeks — the can dated three months out, the half-used bag — goes on that line and onto the [use-first shelf](/pantry-organization/pantry-rotation-system/).

::tip
Update the inventory the same day you put groceries away. If the list is already one shopping trip old, it still catches most problems — the goal is accurate enough to prevent duplicate buys, not perfect.
::

## The weekly pass

Once a week, spend five minutes: cross off what you've used, move anything near its date to "use soon," and note what's low. This is the same 10-minute routine as the [rotation check](/pantry-organization/pantry-rotation-system/), so do them together. The weekly pass keeps the list honest without any heavy lifting.

## The seasonal inventory

Four times a year, do a full inventory alongside the [seasonal pantry cleanout](/pantry-organization/seasonal-pantry-cleanout/). Empty the shelves, check every date, update every line, and reset the use-first shelf. The seasonal pass catches what weekly checks drift past and rebuilds the list from reality instead of memory.

## When the list starts failing

If the inventory stops getting updated, it usually means the zones have drifted — items without a home can't be counted. Fix the layout with the [zones guide](/pantry-organization/pantry-zones-and-layout/), and the list starts working again. The inventory is the thermometer; the zones are the thermostat.
