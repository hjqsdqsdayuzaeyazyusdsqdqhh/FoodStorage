The pantry is where food waste happens in silence. The rice behind the lentils, the canned tomatoes bought "just in case," the flour that survived three moves — all of it was probably fine, and all of it got thrown away because it was invisible and undated.

Organization is not about a beautiful shelf photo. It is about three mechanical systems that make food impossible to forget: zones, FIFO, and labels.

## Zone — everything has a home

Group food by how you use it: baking, canned goods, grains and pasta, breakfast, snacks, oils and condiments. When every category has one shelf, you can see what you have at a glance and you stop buying duplicates of what you already own.

## FIFO — the oldest goes first

FIFO (first in, first out) is the heart of the system. New food goes behind old food, so the oldest is always in front and gets used first. When you open a new bag of rice, decant it, date the container, and place the older container in front. This one habit eliminates most expired-food waste.

## Labels — nothing anonymous

A label with a name and a date turns a mystery jar into a tool. "Flour — opened Jan 15" tells you freshness, rotation order, and what is inside without opening it. Combined with the shelf-life chart, a dated label is the difference between using food and guessing at it.

## Containers — the multiplier

Airtight containers do triple duty: they keep moisture and pantry bugs out, they stack to save space, and they make contents visible. The [pantry container guide](/pantry-organization/pantry-container-guide/) explains what to buy and what to skip.
