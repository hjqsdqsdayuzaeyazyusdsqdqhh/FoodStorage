A container without a label is a guessing game. A container with a name and a date is a tool: you know what's inside, how fresh it is, and which one to use first. The label system is the smallest thing in the pantry and the one that changes everything.

## What to write

The complete pantry label is two items:

1. **The food name** — "Flour", "Brown rice", "Oats"
2. **The fill date** — "06/15" (or "opened 06/15" for refrigerated items)

That's it. Add the best-by date only when it differs from the fill date — useful for items like whole-grain flours where the fill date and expiration are far apart. A name and a date answer every question you'd otherwise have to open the container to ask.

## Where to put it

On the front of the container, on the body — not the lid. Lids get swapped between containers and can't be read on a high shelf. The front label sits at eye level, stays attached to its container through washing, and aligns with the zone system: each shelf reads like a menu of what lives there.

## What to use

The two workable options:

- **Chalk or dry-erase labels** — cheap, rewritable, and perfect for containers you refill. Erase the old date, write the new one, done. Sticks to glass and plastic, wipes off cleanly.
- **Label maker with clear tape** — crisp, permanent, and ideal for matching containers where you want a uniform look. Use clear-backed tape that survives washing.

Skip paper labels and masking tape — they peel, smear, and disintegrate in the dishwasher. If you wash containers by hand, almost any label works; the label maker and chalk options last longest.

::tip
Standardize the date format — "06/15" for June 15 — and use it everywhere. When every date reads the same way, comparing two containers takes one glance, and the [FIFO position rule](/pantry-organization/the-fifo-pantry-system/) does the rest.
::

## Labels are FIFO's engine

The date on the label is what makes rotation automatic. When you fill a container, date it, and place it behind whatever's already open — the older date in front. When you refill, update the date and re-sort. The label system turns "use the oldest first" from a rule you have to remember into something you can read off the shelf.

## When to relabel

Relabel whenever the contents or the date changes: when you refill a container, swap contents, or transfer food between containers. Never leave a label that lies about what's inside. A mismatched label is worse than no label — it leads to mystery baking and expired food. Keep it honest and current.
