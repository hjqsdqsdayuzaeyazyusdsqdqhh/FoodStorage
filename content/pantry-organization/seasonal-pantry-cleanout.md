The seasonal cleanout is the safety net under the whole pantry system. Weekly checks keep things tidy, but once a season you need to pull everything out and look at it honestly — the buried can, the forgotten bag, the system that drifted three inches. Do it four times a year and the pantry never needs the emergency version.

## The checklist

- [ ] **Empty everything** onto the counter, in zones
- [ ] **Sort** into three piles: toss, donate, keep
- [ ] **Check every date** against the shelf-life guides
- [ ] **Inspect** for bugs, damage, and leaks
- [ ] **Wipe all shelves** and let them dry completely
- [ ] **Reset zones** and return food by FIFO, oldest in front
- [ ] **Relabel** containers and refresh the use-first shelf
- [ ] **Update the inventory** for the season ahead

## The three piles

- **Toss** — expired food, damaged packaging, bug-infested bags, anything with mold. Check cans carefully: toss anything bulging, leaking, heavily dented, or rusty — a bulging can is a [botulism warning](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-canned-food-last/) and goes straight in the trash unopened.
- **Donate** — unopened, in-date food you know you won't eat. Local food banks accept most shelf-stable items.
- **Keep** — everything you'll actually use, returned by zone.

## Date-checking with the guides

The cleanout is the one time a year you should actually read the dates. Most pantry food is fine past its date — the question is whether it's past its *quality*. Use the shelf-life guides to make the call: [rice](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-rice-last/), [flour](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-flour-last/), [pasta](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-pasta-last/), and [canned food](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-canned-food-last/) all have different rules. When in doubt, smell, look, and decide with the guide — not the calendar alone.

::tip
Schedule the cleanout on a day you'll already be in the kitchen — the equinoxes, or the same day you change smoke-detector batteries. Attaching it to an existing ritual means it actually happens, four times a year, without needing a reminder.
::

## Resetting the system

A cleanout that doesn't reset the system is wasted effort. As you return food, verify the zones still fit how you cook, put the oldest in front, refresh the [labels](/pantry-organization/how-to-label-pantry-containers/) on anything that needs it, and rebuild the [use-first shelf](/pantry-organization/pantry-rotation-system/). Then update the [pantry inventory](/pantry-organization/pantry-inventory-checklist/) so the next season starts from an accurate list.

## Between cleanouts

The seasonal cleanout is the four-times-a-year reset; the [weekly rotation check](/pantry-organization/pantry-rotation-system/) is the ten-minute line of defense in between. If you do the weekly pass, the cleanouts stay quick — mostly confirmation that the system is working, not damage control.

Start the cleanout with the full [pantry organization system](/pantry-organization/how-to-organize-your-pantry/) if zones and FIFO aren't in place yet.
