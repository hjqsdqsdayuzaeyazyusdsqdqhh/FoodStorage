A pantry with zones is a pantry that uses itself. Without zones, food drifts to wherever it lands, gets buried, and expires. With zones — a fixed home for every category — you can see at a glance what you have, what's low, and what's about to expire.

## The zone map

Start with the zones that match how you actually eat:

- **Canned & jarred** — cans, sauces, broths, pickles
- **Grains & pasta** — rice, pasta, oats, couscous, dried beans
- **Baking** — flour, sugar, leaveners, chocolate, extracts
- **Breakfast** — cereal, granola, breakfast bars, pancake mix
- **Snacks** — chips, crackers, nuts, dried fruit
- **Oils & condiments** — bottles, vinegars, jars
- **Spices** — either a drawer or a door rack
- **Use soon** — a front-and-center shelf for whatever expires soonest

Assign each zone a shelf. The golden rule: every item has exactly one home, and everything always goes back there.

## The shelf-by-shelf layout

Arrange shelves by weight and frequency — the two factors that make a pantry comfortable to use:

- **Bottom shelves:** heavy cans, jars, bottles, large bags of rice and flour. Low and stable means no heavy lifting over your head.
- **Eye level:** what you use every day — breakfast, snacks, oils, and the "use soon" shelf. This is the prime real estate.
- **Top shelves:** light, occasional items — baking supplies, party extras, backups.
- **Door:** spices, oils, condiments, or small jars. The door adds a whole vertical zone a small pantry doesn't otherwise have.

::tip
Make a "use soon" shelf at eye level and put anything approaching its date on it. It's the zone that turns FIFO from a habit into a safety net — you'll reach for that shelf first without thinking.
::

## Zones are for food, not just shelves

The zone system extends to what shouldn't be on the shelves at all. Potatoes, onions, garlic, and winter squash keep best in a cool, dark, ventilated spot — a basket or bin away from tight shelving — and potatoes and onions must be stored apart. Ripening fruit belongs on the counter, not sealed in a pantry. Keeping perishables out of the shelf system is part of the layout, not a violation of it.

## Making it stick

Zones only work if they're consistent. Two rules keep them that way: return everything to its zone after every use, and put new purchases in the back behind older stock (FIFO). When a zone starts to overflow, that's the signal — either the zone is in the wrong place, or you're overstocked. Adjust the map, don't abandon it.
