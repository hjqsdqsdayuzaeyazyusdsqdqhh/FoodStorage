Every pantry staple has the same enemies — heat, light, moisture, air, and bugs — and the same defenses: airtight containers, a cool dry pantry, and rotation. Learn the pattern once and you can store anything in the pantry correctly, because the rules barely change between staples.

## The universal rules

- **Temperature:** 50–70°F, away from the stove, oven, and fridge exhaust. Above 75°F, quality declines fast for oils, whole grains, and cans.
- **Moisture:** none. Airtight containers and a dry location keep out the moisture that causes mold and clumping.
- **Light:** none. The dark of a closed cabinet beats an open shelf near a window.
- **Air:** sealed. Oxygen causes rancidity in whole grains and oils, and staleness in everything.
- **Rotation:** oldest first. [FIFO](/pantry-organization/the-fifo-pantry-system/) keeps the shelf moving so nothing expires.

## Staple by staple

- **Rice** — white rice in an airtight container lasts 2+ years; brown rice goes in the fridge or freezer (its bran oils go rancid in months). See [how long rice lasts](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-rice-last/).
- **Flour** — white flour: airtight, 6–8 months. Whole-wheat flour: fridge or freezer, 1–2 years. See [how long flour lasts](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-flour-last/).
- **Pasta** — airtight, 1–2 years. Whole-grain pasta only 3–6 months. See [how long pasta lasts](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-pasta-last/).
- **Sugar** — airtight; sugar doesn't spoil but hardens without a seal. **Beans** — airtight, years; inspect for holes and bugs.
- **Oils** — cool and dark, away from the stove; nut oils in the fridge. Opened oil keeps 3–6 months; rancid oil smells bitter-nutty and goes out.
- **Canned goods** — cool, dry, below 75°F, never over the stove; check cans for bulges and dents. See [how long canned food lasts](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-canned-food-last/).

::tip
Freeze every new bag of rice, flour, or beans for 3 days before moving it to a container. It kills any pantry-moth eggs that came with the bag — the single most common way bugs enter a pantry.
::

## The pantry setup

Arrange the staples by zone — canned together, grains together, baking together — with the oldest in front. Put heavy bags and cans on low shelves, everyday staples at eye level, and the [use-first shelf](/pantry-organization/pantry-rotation-system/) in front. Label every container with a name and date, per the [label guide](/pantry-organization/how-to-label-pantry-containers/).

## When to upgrade storage

Buy better containers when you notice: staples going stale before you finish them, bugs appearing, or bags that never close properly. The [pantry container guide](/pantry-organization/pantry-container-guide/) walks through what to buy for each staple. A good container setup pays for itself in the food it saves within months.
