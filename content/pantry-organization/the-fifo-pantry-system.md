Most pantry waste is not bad food. It's perfectly good food that got buried behind newer purchases and expired before anyone found it. FIFO — first in, first out — is the mechanical fix: the oldest food always sits in front, so it always gets used first. No remembering, no labels-reading, just one rule.

## The FIFO rule

First in, first out. The oldest item goes in front. The newest goes in back. When you use something, take from the front. When you buy something, put it in the back. When you refill a container, the older container goes in front.

That's the entire system. It works because it doesn't rely on memory — the arrangement itself tells you what to use next.

## The two actions that make it work

**New purchases go behind.** A new jar of peanut butter goes behind the older one, not in front. A new bag of rice goes behind the bag you're already using. This single action, done every shopping trip, keeps the oldest stock permanently in front.

**Opened containers get dates.** When you open a new bag of flour or pour rice into a container, write the food name and the date on it, and place it behind whatever's already open. A date label turns a mystery jar into a tool: you can see which is older at a glance, without sniffing.

::tip
When you bring groceries home, put the new stuff in the back before you put anything away. If you put new food in front "for convenience," you've defeated FIFO in the first ten seconds. Back-first is the whole trick.
::

## Where FIFO matters most

- **Canned goods** — [canned food lasts for years](/shelf-life/pantry/how-long-does-canned-food-last/), but only if it's eaten. FIFO keeps the oldest cans rotating into meals.
- **Baking supplies** — flour, sugar, and leaveners go stale or lose potency. FIFO keeps the oldest in play.
- **Snacks and breakfast** — the highest-turnover zones, and the easiest to clutter. FIFO stops the "three half-empty bags" problem.
- **Spices** — old spices lose their punch. FIFO keeps the oldest jar in front so it gets used.

## The weekly FIFO routine

FIFO maintenance is a 10-minute habit: once a week, scan each zone for anything that's moved to the back that should be in front, and pull older items forward. Check dates on the front row as you go. That's it. Combined with the [pantry inventory checklist](/pantry-organization/pantry-inventory-checklist/) and a [seasonal cleanout](/pantry-organization/seasonal-pantry-cleanout/), FIFO keeps the pantry permanently waste-free.
