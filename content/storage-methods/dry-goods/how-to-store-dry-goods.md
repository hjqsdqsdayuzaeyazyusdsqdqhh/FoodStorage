Dry goods are the easiest food in the house to store — and the most likely to be stored badly. The enemies are the same four for everything: moisture, bugs, heat, and time. Learn the pattern once and rice, flour, pasta, sugar, and beans all follow the same rules.

## Step 1 — Choose airtight containers

The container is the whole game. Move every staple out of its original bag or box into an airtight container with a gasket seal. Paper bags breathe — they let in moisture and pantry moths. A sealed container blocks both, and it blocks oxygen, which slows staleness and rancidity.

- **Glass** for flour and sugar — non-porous, no odor retention, easy to keep clean.
- **BPA-free plastic** for rice, pasta, and beans — light, stackable, shatterproof.
- **Food-grade buckets** for bulk and long-term storage — with oxygen absorbers for years-long storage.

## Step 2 — Store in a cool, dark place

Temperature is the silent killer of dry goods. Above 75°F, oils go rancid, whole grains stale, and cans degrade faster. Keep staples at 50–70°F in a dark pantry or cabinet — never over the stove, above the fridge, or in direct sunlight. Moisture is the other half: the pantry must be dry, and every container must seal.

::tip
Check the environment, not just the containers: a cabinet over the dishwasher or next to the stove is too warm and too humid, even with perfect containers. Move the dry-goods zone to a cool, dry, dark spot and everything lasts measurably longer.
::

## Step 3 — Freeze new grains

Bugs in the pantry almost always arrive in a package, not spontaneously. Rice, flour, and beans can carry pantry-moth eggs that hatch weeks later. Freeze every new bag for 3 days before moving it to its container — the cold kills the eggs. Then inspect as you fill: webbing, bugs, or stuck-together grains mean the bag goes out.

## Step 4 — Label and rotate

Label every container with the food name and the fill date, then rotate [FIFO](/pantry-organization/the-fifo-pantry-system/) — oldest in front, newest in back. A dated label tells you what's inside and which to use first, no opening required. The [label guide](/pantry-organization/how-to-label-pantry-containers/) has the full system.

## Step 5 — Store whole grains cold

The one exception to the pantry rule: whole-grain products. Brown rice, whole-wheat flour, and rolled oats contain the oily germ of the grain, which goes rancid in months at room temperature. Store them in the fridge or freezer in airtight containers, where they keep 6–12 months or longer. Let cold flour warm to room temperature before baking.

## Dry goods shelf life at a glance

Stored airtight at 50–70°F: white rice 2+ years, pasta 1–2 years, white flour 6–8 months, sugar years, dried beans years, canned food 2–5 years. Whole grains 3–6 months at room temperature, 6–12 months cold. See the [shelf-life chart](/shelf-life/) for every food's exact window.
