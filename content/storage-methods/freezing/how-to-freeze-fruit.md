Frozen fruit is a year-round flavor hack. Berries at their summer peak, bananas that would have gone brown, mango on sale — frozen properly, they keep their flavor for months and turn into smoothies, crisps, and jams on demand. The whole method is short, and one step — flash freezing — makes it look professional.

## Step 1 — Wash and dry

Wash the fruit and dry it thoroughly. Drying matters: surface water turns to ice and makes pieces stick together. Remove stems, pits, and peels now, so the bag is ready to pour into the blender or pan. For berries, this is also the moment to pick out any bruised or moldy ones — one bad berry ruins a bag.

## Step 2 — Cut to size

Slice larger fruit — bananas, mango, peaches, pineapple — into the sizes you'll use. Bananas for smoothies can go in halves or chunks; peaches and mango work best sliced. Berries and grapes stay whole. Cutting before freezing means even freezing and exactly the portion you want later.

## Step 3 — Flash freeze

This is the step that separates clumps from perfect: lay the fruit in a single layer on a baking sheet lined with parchment, and freeze until solid — about 1–2 hours. Individually frozen pieces pour out like store-bought frozen fruit, and you can grab a handful without thawing the whole bag.

::tip
Freeze fruit on a parchment-lined sheet in a single layer, and keep pieces from touching. It takes an extra hour and turns a solid ice block into a bag of loose, grab-able fruit — the difference is worth it every time.
::

## Step 4 — Pack airtight

Pour the frozen pieces into airtight freezer bags, press out as much air as possible, and label with the fruit and the date. A vacuum sealer is ideal but not required — pressing the air out of a good freezer bag works for the standard 8–12 month window.

## Step 5 — Store at 0°F

Keep frozen fruit at 0°F or below. Use it straight from the freezer — no thawing — in smoothies, sauces, and baking. For pies and crisps, frozen fruit often works better than fresh because it releases less liquid as it bakes.

## Fruit-by-fruit notes

- **Berries** — flash freeze whole; use straight from the freezer.
- **Bananas** — peel and freeze chunks; perfect for smoothies and nice cream.
- **Peaches & pears** — toss with lemon juice or sugar to prevent browning.
- **Mango & pineapple** — slice and flash freeze.
- **Citrus** — freeze segments or juice in ice-cube trays.
- **Watermelon & melon** — freeze for smoothies only; they thaw watery.

## What frozen fruit is not for

Frozen fruit thaws soft — ice crystals burst the cell walls — so it's not for the fruit bowl. It's for smoothies, baking, sauces, jams, and frozen treats. That's the tradeoff that makes it a bargain: buy in season, freeze at peak, and use it in dishes where soft is exactly right.
