Freezing is the best tool a meat-eater has, and the only way to ruin it is air. Frozen meat at 0°F stays safe indefinitely — the entire game is quality, and quality is determined by how you package it. Do it right once and you can buy in bulk, cook on schedule, and never taste the freezer.

## Step 1 — Start with fresh meat

Freeze meat within 1–2 days of purchase, while it's at its peak. The freezer preserves quality — it doesn't restore it — so freezing a cut that's already past its prime gives you a freezer full of disappointment. Only freeze raw meat that has never been thawed, unless it was thawed in the refrigerator.

## Step 2 — Portion before freezing

Divide the meat into meal-sized portions before you freeze it. A whole package that freezes as one block means thawing a block when you wanted two steaks. Freeze portions flat — steak, ground beef, and chicken in a single layer — so they freeze faster and thaw in a fraction of the time. Flat-frozen meat stacks too, which keeps the freezer organized.

## Step 3 — Wrap airtight

Air is the enemy. It causes freezer burn — dry, gray, leathery patches where moisture evaporates from the meat's surface — and it oxidizes the meat's flavor. Three ways to win:

1. **Freezer bags** — press every bit of air out before sealing.
2. **Plastic wrap** — wrap tightly against the meat, then add a foil layer.
3. **Vacuum sealer** — the gold standard; removes air entirely and roughly doubles quality life.

For anything frozen more than a month, use a second layer. Label everything — you will not remember what that block is in three months.

::tip
A wet-wipe trick for freezer bags: dip a paper towel in water, leave it on the counter, and press the bag down as you seal — or simply press the bag against a bowl of water to push the air out. The goal is zero air around the meat.
::

## Step 4 — Label and rotate

Every package gets three things in marker: the meat type, the cut, and the date. Then rotate [FIFO](/pantry-organization/the-fifo-pantry-system/) — oldest in front, newest in back — so the oldest meat gets eaten first. Without labels, the freezer becomes archaeology; with them, it becomes a menu.

## Step 5 — Freeze at 0°F and thaw safely

Freeze at 0°F or below, placing fresh packages flat in the coldest, most open spot so they freeze fast. Thaw meat three safe ways only:

- **Refrigerator** (best) — plan ahead; a small package thaws overnight, a large roast takes a day or two.
- **Cold water** — sealed in a leak-proof bag, changing the water every 30 minutes.
- **Microwave** — only if you'll cook the meat immediately after.

::safety
Never thaw meat on the counter. The outside reaches the 40–140°F danger zone while the inside is still frozen, and bacteria multiply in the warm outer layer. Cook or refrigerate thawed meat promptly, and cook ground meat to 160°F, poultry to 165°F.
::

## Freezer storage times

Best-quality windows at 0°F: raw chicken 9–12 months, raw beef and pork 6–12 months, ground meat 3–4 months, fish and shellfish 2–3 months, cooked meat 3–4 months. Beyond these windows the meat is still safe — just lower in quality. See the [frozen food guide](/shelf-life/freezer/) for the complete chart.

For the packaging that stops freezer burn entirely, see [how to prevent freezer burn](/storage-methods/freezing/how-to-prevent-freezer-burn/).
