Vegetables freeze brilliantly if you respect the one rule that separates good from mushy: most vegetables need blanching first. Blanching is a 5-minute stop that kills the enzymes that would otherwise fade, soften, and stale the vegetables over months in the freezer. Skip it and your "fresh" frozen broccoli comes out gray and bland.

## Step 1 — Choose the right vegetables

The freezer loves firm, starchy, and low-moisture vegetables: broccoli, cauliflower, green beans, corn, carrots, peas, spinach, kale, and root vegetables. It hates high-water ones: lettuce, cucumbers, radishes, and celery turn to mush because ice crystals rupture their cell walls. Tomatoes freeze only for cooking. Bell peppers and onions freeze beautifully raw and chopped.

## Step 2 — Wash and cut

Wash everything, trim the ends, and cut vegetables into the sizes you'll cook with — florets, slices, or diced. Cutting before freezing means even freezing and even thawing, and it means the bag is ready to pour straight into the pan. Blanching times depend on piece size, so cut first, then measure.

## Step 3 — Blanch in boiling water

Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Drop the vegetables in and blanch for the right time — about 2 minutes for leafy greens, 3 for green beans, broccoli, and carrots, 3–4 for corn — then immediately plunge them into a bowl of ice water for the same length of time. The ice bath stops the cooking instantly. Drain well.

::tip
Blanch in a strainer or basket so you can lift everything out at once and drop it straight into the ice bath. Keep a timer going — under-blanching doesn't fully stop the enzymes, and over-blanching turns vegetables soft.
::

## Step 4 — Dry and pack

Moisture is the enemy of frozen vegetables — it turns into ice crystals that puncture texture. Drain thoroughly, pat dry with a towel, then pack into airtight freezer bags, pressing out as much air as possible. Label each bag with the vegetable and the date. For the longest quality life, use a vacuum sealer.

## Step 5 — Freeze flat at 0°F

Freeze bags flat in a single layer so the pieces freeze individually instead of in a solid block. For berries and loose pieces, "flash freeze" on a baking sheet first, then pour into a bag. Store at 0°F or below. Properly frozen vegetables keep best quality for 8–12 months.

## Vegetables that skip the blanch

You can skip blanching for a short list: bell peppers and onions (chop and freeze raw), corn kernels (freeze raw, cook later), and herbs (chop, freeze in oil or water in an ice-cube tray). Tomatoes freeze whole for cooking. Everything else earns its blanching.

## How to use frozen vegetables

Frozen vegetables are the fastest shortcut in the kitchen — no prep, no waste, and they're packed at peak freshness. Add them straight from the freezer to stir-fries, soups, and roasts (cooking from frozen preserves texture better than thawing first). Slightly increase cooking time for dense vegetables, and season generously — freezing mutes flavor a touch.
