Freezing is the closest thing to a time machine your kitchen has. At 0°F, bacteria stop growing entirely — the food does not spoil, it simply waits. That is why the "shelf life" of frozen food is really a quality question, not a safety one.

The two rules that govern the whole freezer: keep it at 0°F or below, and keep air off the food. Temperature is your safety; airtight wrapping is your quality.

## Why food loses quality in the freezer

Two things happen to frozen food over months. First, ice crystals grow and puncture cell walls, so watery foods — berries, tomatoes, cooked pasta — turn mushy when thawed. Second, moisture evaporates from any exposed surface into the cold air, leaving dry, gray, leathery patches: freezer burn. Both are quality problems, not safety problems. You prevent them by freezing fast, in airtight packaging, with as much air removed as possible.

## Recommended freezer times (best quality)

- **Raw poultry:** 9 months for parts, 12 months for a whole bird.
- **Beef and pork roasts/steaks:** 6–12 months.
- **Ground meat:** 3–4 months.
- **Fish and shellfish:** 2–3 months.
- **Fruit and vegetables:** 8–12 months.
- **Cooked leftovers:** 2–6 months.
- **Bread and rolls:** 3 months (best texture); safe for 6.

## Power outage: the 48-hour rule

If the power goes out, do not open the freezer. A full freezer keeps food safe for about 48 hours; a half-full one for about 24 hours. Afterward, check temperatures: if the freezer rose above 40°F for more than 2 hours, throw away the meat, poultry, fish, and dairy. Keep the door shut and the food stays safe.
