Dairy is the most-bought category in most kitchens and the one people trust least. The good news: milk does not spoil the moment the date passes, and eggs keep for weeks — if the fridge stays at 40°F or below.

The single biggest mistake people make with dairy is the fridge door. The door is the warmest part of the fridge, swinging open and closed all day. Milk, yogurt, and eggs belong on a middle or lower shelf in the back, not in the door shelf. It takes no time to move them, and it buys you the full storage window.

## What the dates actually mean

A best-by date on dairy is peak-quality guidance, not a poison alert. Milk past its date can still be fine for days if it smells and tastes normal. What you are really looking for is spoilage: a sour smell, curdling, or mold. When the smell is off, throw it out — the cost of one carton is nothing compared to a foodborne illness.

## Freezing dairy

Butter and hard cheese freeze beautifully for months. Milk freezes but separates slightly — thaw it in the fridge and shake well, then use it for cooking. Yogurt and soft cheese turn watery and grainy, so freeze them only if you plan to bake or blend. Every frozen dairy item stays safe at 0°F indefinitely; the limit is quality.

## Eggs need refrigeration

US commercial eggs are washed, which strips their natural protective coating, so they must stay refrigerated at 40°F or below. Stored that way, eggs keep 3–5 weeks from the pack date — long past the printed date. Never leave them out for more than 2 hours, and keep them in the carton, not the door.
