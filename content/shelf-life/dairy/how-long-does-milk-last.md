Milk is the most-bought food in most kitchens and the one that gets thrown away the most. The truth is more forgiving than most people think: milk does not die on its sell-by date, and a carton you thought was spoiled is often still fine.

The two rules that matter: keep the fridge at 40°F or below, and keep the carton closed. Temperature is everything with milk — the difference between a carton that lasts a week past its date and one that sours in three days is almost always the fridge shelf it lives on.

## How long milk lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge (unopened) | 5–7 days past printed date | Keep at 40°F or below |
| Fridge (opened) | About 1 week | Reseal tightly; store on a shelf, not the door |
| Room temperature | 2 hours max (1 hour above 90°F) | Discard after the limit — reheating doesn't help |
| Freezer | 3 months (best quality) | Thaw in the fridge; shake well before use |

## Storage conditions that actually matter

The single biggest mistake is the fridge door. The door is the warmest part of the refrigerator, and it swings open dozens of times a day, so the temperature there can swing by 10 degrees or more. Milk, yogurt, and eggs belong on a middle or lower shelf in the back, where the cold is stable.

Keep the carton closed whenever possible — every open lets warm air in and speeds up spoilage. And pour from a clean spout or glass: backwash from a used cup puts bacteria into the carton and visibly shortens its life.

::tip
Buy the smallest size you will actually drink, and store it on the coldest shelf — not the door. A half-gallon you finish is cheaper than a gallon you pour down the sink.
::

## How to tell milk has gone bad

The nose is the best tool. Sour, acrid, or barnyard smell means the bacteria count is already high — discard it. If the smell passes but the taste is off, the carton is past its prime. Visible curdling, a lumpy texture, or a yellowed separation are all definite signs of spoilage. When in doubt, throw it out.

::safety
Never taste milk to "check" it once the smell is off. One sip of badly spoiled milk is unlikely to be dangerous, but it is not worth the gamble — and it is a bad habit for foods that carry real risk. Smell first, always.
::

## Does milk freeze well?

Yes, with a texture trade-off. Milk freezes safely at 0°F for up to 3 months and stays safe indefinitely beyond that. Freezing separates the fat, which makes thawed milk slightly grainy — fine for cooking, baking, and smoothies, less ideal for drinking straight. Thaw in the refrigerator, not the counter, and shake or stir vigorously before use. Freeze only in containers with headroom: milk expands.

## Freezing tips

- Freeze in portions you will use at once, since refreezing thawed milk is not recommended.
- Leave about an inch of headroom in the container — milk expands as it freezes.
- Label with the date; use frozen milk within 3 months for best quality.
