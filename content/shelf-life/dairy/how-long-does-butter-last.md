    a: Butter keeps 1–3 months in the refrigerator at 40°F or below, and often several weeks past the printed date if the wrapper stays intact. Store it in its original wrapper inside the butter compartment or an airtight container so it doesn't absorb fridge odors.
  - q: Can butter be left on the counter?
    a: Salted butter can sit at room temperature for a day or two in a covered butter dish for easy spreading — but only salted butter, and only for a few days. Unsalted butter goes rancid much faster at room temperature and should stay refrigerated. If the kitchen runs warm or humid, keep even salted butter cold.
  - q: How long does butter last in the freezer?
    a: Butter freezes beautifully for 6–9 months, and stays safe far longer — butter is the most freezer-friendly dairy product. Wrap it tightly in its original packaging plus a layer of plastic or foil to block freezer air and odors, and thaw in the fridge. Frozen butter grates directly into dough without thawing.
  - q: How can you tell if butter has gone bad?
    a: Spoiled butter smells sour, cheesy, or rancid — like stale nuts or old paint — and develops an off, bitter taste. Fresh butter is mild, sweet, and creamy. Any dark, moldy spots, a slimy surface, or a musty odor mean it's past its prime. Butter is made from cream, so rancidity is the main thing to watch for.
  - q: Does salted or unsalted butter last longer?
    a: Salted butter. Salt draws out moisture and inhibits the bacteria that cause spoilage, so salted butter keeps noticeably longer in the fridge and is the only butter safe on the counter. Butter is the most forgiving food in the dairy aisle. It lasts months in the fridge, most of a year in the freezer, and — for salted butter — a few days on the counter. About the only way to ruin it is to leave it out too long, or leave it open in the fridge until it tastes like everything else in there.

## How long butter lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter (salted butter) | 1–2 days | Covered butter dish; cooler kitchens only |
| Counter (unsalted butter) | Avoid | Goes rancid quickly at room temperature |
| Fridge | 1–3 months | Keep in original wrapper, sealed |
| Freezer | 6–9 months (best quality) | Wrap airtight; safe much longer |
| Room temperature (warm kitchen) | A few hours | Keep cold if your kitchen runs warm |

## Storage conditions for butter

Butter's enemies are air, light, and fridge odors. Air oxidizes the milk fat and turns it rancid; light speeds that oxidation; and butter absorbs the smell of whatever is next to it. That is why the original foil wrapper matters: keep butter in it, folded closed, or move it to an airtight butter keeper.

The butter compartment in the fridge door is fine — unlike milk, butter tolerates the door because it is not the most sensitive dairy. If you keep both salted and unsalted, mark them: they are easy to confuse and the shelf lives differ.

::tip
Buy salted butter for the table and unsalted for baking, then freeze the unsalted in its box. Frozen unsalted butter grates straight into scones and pastry — no thawing, and no fridge rancidity.
::

## How to tell butter has gone bad

The tell is the smell. Fresh butter smells sweet and mild; spoiled butter smells sour, cheesy, or rancid — like stale nuts or old paint. Taste confirms it: rancid butter tastes sharp and bitter even in small amounts.

Check the surface too. Dark yellowing, a slimy film, or mold spots on butter mean it's past its prime. If only the very edge is discolored, trim it off — the interior is fine. If the smell is off anywhere, discard the whole stick.

::note
Butter that smells fine but tastes slightly flat or stale is not dangerous — it's just old. Use it for cooking where the flavor won't matter, and buy smaller amounts next time. Rancid butter, however, tastes bad enough to ruin whatever it touches, so throw it out.
::

## Freezing butter

Butter is the most freezer-friendly dairy: freeze it for 6–9 months with no real quality loss. Wrap it in its original packaging plus a layer of plastic wrap or foil so it doesn't pick up freezer odors, and it will keep far beyond the 9-month quality window. Thaw in the fridge for a few hours, or grate frozen butter straight into dough — it is a baker's secret weapon for flaky pastry.
