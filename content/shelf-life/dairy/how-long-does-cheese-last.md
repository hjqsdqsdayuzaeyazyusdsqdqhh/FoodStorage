Cheese is the rare dairy product that gets better with a little attention. Stored badly, it dries out, sweats, and turns bitter. Stored well — in paper, in the right drawer — a block of hard cheese stays delicious for weeks.

The rule that explains everything: cheese is alive. It needs to breathe but not dry out. Tight plastic wrap suffocates it and traps the moisture that breeds mold; leaving it bare dries it into a crumbly shell. Paper wrap with a loose plastic covering is the sweet spot.

## How long cheese lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge — hard cheese (opened) | 3–4 weeks | Rewrap in fresh paper after each use |
| Fridge — semi-soft (mozzarella) | About 2 weeks | Keep in brine or fresh paper |
| Fridge — soft cheese (brie, feta, cream cheese) | 1–2 weeks | Throw out if mold appears |
| Fridge — unopened hard cheese | 2–6 months | Store in the original packaging |
| Freezer — grated hard cheese | Up to 6 months | Grate before freezing; thaw in fridge |
| Room temperature | 2 hours max for service | Return to fridge promptly |

## Storage conditions for cheese

Cheese belongs in the warmest part of the fridge — usually the vegetable or cheese drawer — not the door. The door's temperature swings cause condensation, and condensation is mold fuel.

Wrap cheese in waxed or parchment paper first, then tuck it loosely in a plastic bag or container so it can still breathe. Rewrap in fresh paper after every use: old paper holds the moisture that makes cheese sweat. For grated and sliced cheese, the fridge-life is shorter — moisture and surface area mean faster spoilage — so buy those in small amounts.

::tip
Grate a large block of cheddar or Parmesan and freeze the grated cheese in a zip-top bag. It thaws in minutes, cooks identically, and gives you hard-cheese convenience at block-cheese prices.
::

## Mold: when to cut and when to throw

Hard and semi-hard cheese — cheddar, Parmesan, Swiss, Gouda — is dense enough that mold cannot penetrate. Cut off at least 1 inch around and below the mold spot, rewrap the rest, and it is safe to eat.

Soft and shredded cheese is the opposite. Brie, feta, ricotta, cream cheese, and pre-shredded blends are moist enough that mold spores run through the whole piece. Any visible mold means the entire package goes in the bin.

::warning
Never just scrape mold off soft cheese and eat the rest. In soft, high-moisture cheeses, mold roots below the visible surface where you cannot see it. When in doubt on any soft cheese, discard.
::

## Does cheese freeze well?

Hard cheese freezes well and soft cheese does not. Grated hard cheese freezes for up to 6 months and thaws in minutes — ideal for casseroles, sauces, and pizza. Freezing whole blocks is fine for cooking too, though the texture turns crumbly, so skip it for cheese boards. Freeze cheese at 0°F, wrapped tightly to prevent freezer burn, and thaw in the fridge overnight.
