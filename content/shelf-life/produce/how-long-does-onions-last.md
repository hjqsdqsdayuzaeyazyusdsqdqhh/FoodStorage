  - q: How long do cut onions last in the fridge?
    a: Cut onions keep 7–10 days in the refrigerator in a sealed container or bag. They last longest if you peel and chop the whole onion and store the pieces, since a cut, half-peeled onion dries out. If any cut onion develops a slimy layer or off smell, trim or discard it.

Onions are the kitchen's long-distance runner among vegetables — a good storage onion will wait for you for two months. The catch is that onions are the opposite of potatoes in almost every way, and storing them the same way is the most common mistake in the produce aisle.

## How long onions last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pantry (whole) | 1–2 months | Mesh bag or open basket, dry and ventilated |
| Pantry (sweet onions) | 2–4 weeks | Sweeter onions hold more moisture |
| Fridge (cut) | 7–10 days | Sealed container; peel and chop first |
| Freezer (chopped) | 3–6 months | Use straight from the freezer for cooking |

## Storage conditions for onions

Whole onions want a cool, dark, dry, and ventilated spot — a mesh bag, open basket, or single layer in a cabinet. Light makes them sprout and green, moisture makes them rot, and a sealed plastic bag traps exactly that moisture. Sweet onions like Vidalia and Walla Walla are the exception: they're juicier, so they spoil faster and some cooks refrigerate them.

The golden rule is separation. Onions and potatoes actively spoil each other — onions release moisture and potatoes release ethylene gas, and each accelerates the other's decay. Store them in different cabinets, or at least different baskets.

::tip
For cut onions, peel and chop the entire onion and store the pieces in a sealed container. A lone half-onion wrapped in plastic dries out and goes limp fast; chopped pieces in an airtight container hold up for the full 7–10 days.
::

## How to tell onions are past their prime

Look for soft, mushy spots, a strong sweet-rotten smell, visible mold (usually black or gray), or green sprouts. Sprouted onions are fine to eat — trim the shoots — but use them fast, since the sprout steals flavor and crunch. An onion that has gone soft, smells fermented, or shows mold goes in the compost.

## Freezing onions

Chopped onions freeze beautifully. Peel, chop, and freeze in zip-top bags for 3–6 months, and use them straight from the freezer in any cooked dish — no thawing needed. Frozen onions lose their crisp bite, so save them for soups, stews, sauces, and sautés rather than salads. They're a huge convenience: chop a batch on prep day, freeze it flat, and break off what you need.
