Lettuce is the canary in the crisper drawer — it wilts before anything else, and it wilts fast. But the wilting is not random, and it's not usually the lettuce's fault. Wilting is moisture loss, and moisture loss is a storage problem you can fix in about thirty seconds.

## How long lettuce lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge (crisper drawer) | 5–7 days | Bag or container that holds moisture |
| Fridge (sturdy varieties) | 10–12 days | Romaine and iceberg last longest |
| Counter | A few hours | Warmth and open air wilt quickly |
| Freezer | Not recommended | Turns to mush when thawed |

## Storage conditions for lettuce

Lettuce wants three things: cold, humid, and breathable. The crisper drawer provides the cold and humidity; a plastic bag or a container with a damp paper towel provides the breathable moisture. The classic setup: wash and dry the lettuce, store it in a bag lined with a paper towel to absorb excess moisture, and keep the bag slightly open for airflow.

Wash lettuce only when you're about to use it. Bagged greens labeled "pre-washed" are already clean — washing them again adds surface water that speeds spoilage. And do not store lettuce next to ethylene emitters like apples and bananas; ethylene speeds the yellowing and softening of greens.

::tip
Keep a paper towel inside your lettuce bag and swap it when it's wet. The towel absorbs the condensation that makes leaves go slimy, and the greens stay crisp an extra few days for zero effort.
::

## How to tell lettuce is past its prime

Wilted lettuce is recoverable; slimy lettuce is not. If leaves are limp, revive them in a bowl of ice water for 10–30 minutes, spin dry, and use right away. If leaves are slimy, yellowed, mushy, or give off a sour, fermented smell — or have brown spots that turn to rot — the lettuce is done. Bagged greens in particular spoil fast once they start, so check them daily.

## Can you freeze lettuce?

No — not for eating fresh. Lettuce is so full of water that freezing ruptures its cell walls and turns it into mush. The only way to freeze it usefully is blanched for soups and stews, where the texture change doesn't matter. If you want frozen greens for smoothies and cooking, spinach and kale freeze far better than lettuce.
