Strawberries are the shortest-lived fruit in the store and the one most people store wrong. The secret is almost a contradiction: wash them less, not more. Berries carry mold spores on their skin and hold a lot of moisture, so the single thing that makes them last is keeping them dry and cold.

## How long strawberries last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter | 1 day | Berries perish fast at room temperature |
| Fridge (unwashed) | 3–7 days | Original carton, paper towel, crisper drawer |
| Fridge (washed) | 1–2 days | Wash only before eating |
| Freezer | 8–12 months | Wash, hull, freeze on a tray first |

## Storage conditions for strawberries

The system is: cold, dry, and spaced. Put strawberries in the fridge immediately, in their original ventilated carton lined with a paper towel to absorb moisture. Place them in the crisper drawer where humidity is controlled. Do not stack berries in a deep airtight container — that traps the moisture and bruises the fruit, both of which breed mold.

Do not wash them until you're ready to eat them. Water on the surface is exactly what mold needs. If you buy strawberries with a bruised or leaky one in the carton, remove it right away — one bad berry goes to fuzzy green in a day and takes its neighbors with it.

::tip
When you get berries home, do a five-second sort: remove any bruised, leaky, or moldy berry and the paper towel they came on. That single habit is the biggest difference between berries that last a day and berries that last a week.
::

## How to tell strawberries are past their prime

Mold is the big one: fuzzy gray or green patches mean that berry goes. Remove moldy berries immediately, because spores spread. A strawberry past its prime is mushy, wrinkled, or dull red, with a soft, leaky base or an off, fermenting smell. Small soft spots are fine if you're using them the same day — just cut them out.

## Freezing strawberries

Frozen strawberries are a freezer staple. Wash and thoroughly dry them, hull them, and freeze them in a single layer on a tray before transferring to a zip-top bag. Frozen individually, they won't clump and you can grab a handful. They keep 8–12 months and are perfect for smoothies, jam, sauces, and baking. Like all berries, they soften when thawed, so plan for cooking, not snacking.
