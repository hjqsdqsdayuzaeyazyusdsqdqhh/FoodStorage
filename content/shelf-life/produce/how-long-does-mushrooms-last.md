Mushrooms are the driest-looking food that actually despises dry air, and the one most people suffocate in plastic. They're about 90% water, so everything comes down to one balance: keep them from drying out without trapping the moisture that turns them slimy. A paper bag in the crisper drawer is the whole system.

## How long mushrooms last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter | 1 day | Deteriorate fast at room temperature |
| Fridge (whole, paper bag) | 3–7 days | Paper bag in the crisper drawer |
| Fridge (sliced) | 3–5 days | Loosely covered container |
| Freezer (cooked) | 8–12 months | Sauté or roast first |

## Storage conditions for mushrooms

Refrigerate mushrooms immediately in their original packaging or a paper bag, in the crisper drawer. Paper is the trick: it lets moisture escape while keeping the mushrooms from drying out. If you only have plastic, leave the bag open or poke holes. Handle mushrooms as little as possible — bruising releases moisture, and moisture is what leads to slime.

Do not wash mushrooms until you're about to use them. Mushrooms absorb water like sponges, and wet mushrooms go slimy fast. Wipe them with a dry paper towel or give them a quick rinse and thorough pat-dry right before cooking — never before storage.

::tip
Buy mushrooms loose, not pre-sliced, if you can — whole mushrooms keep a full week while sliced ones last half that. Slice them at cooking time and store the whole ones in a paper bag.
::

## How to tell mushrooms are past their prime

Fresh mushrooms are firm, dry, and smell mild and earthy. Warning signs: a slimy or sticky surface, wrinkles and shriveling, dark or mushy bruises, and a sour, musty smell — the "mushroom gone wrong" odor. Slight darkening of the gills is normal aging, not spoilage. Once mushrooms are slimy or smell off, they're done; compost them.

## Freezing mushrooms

Freeze mushrooms cooked, not raw. Raw mushrooms are mostly water and turn into mush; sautéed or roasted mushrooms freeze beautifully. Cook them until the liquid evaporates, cool completely, freeze flat on a tray, then transfer to an airtight bag. They keep 8–12 months and go straight from the freezer into soups, sauces, risotto, and stir-fries.
