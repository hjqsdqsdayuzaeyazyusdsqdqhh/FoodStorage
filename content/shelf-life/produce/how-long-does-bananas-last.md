faq:
  - q: How long do bananas last on the counter?
    a: Bananas ripen on the counter in 3–5 days at room temperature. Warm kitchens ripen them faster, cool rooms slower. Once they reach the ripeness you like, move them to the fridge — the skin will turn brown or black, but the fruit inside stays good for 3–7 more days.
  - q: Can you put bananas in the fridge?
    a: Yes, once they are ripe. The fridge stops ripening almost entirely, so a ripe banana stays good for about a week in the fridge. The skin turns brown or black — that's normal and just cosmetic — but the flesh stays firm and sweet. Green bananas will not ripen properly in the fridge.
  - q: Why do bananas ripen so fast and turn everything else brown?
    a: Bananas release ethylene gas, a natural ripening hormone. It ripens the banana itself and also ripens nearby fruit — apples, avocados, tomatoes — which is why one banana in a fruit bowl turns everything faster. Store bananas away from other produce, or hang them, to slow the chain reaction.
  - q: Can you freeze bananas?
    a: Yes. Peel ripe bananas, cut them in half or slice them, and freeze in zip-top bags for 2–3 months. Frozen bananas are perfect for smoothies, nice cream, and baking. No need to thaw for smoothies — throw them straight in. Thawed bananas turn brown and soft, so use frozen ones for cooking, not snacking.
  - q: How can you tell if a banana has gone bad?
    a: Ripe bananas have brown spots — that's fine. A banana is past its prime when the flesh is mushy, brown, and fermented-smelling, or when liquid seeps from the stem. Check for mold at the stem and on the skin creases. Very brown, soft, or oozing bananas go to the compost or the banana bread.

Bananas are the fruit that never waits for you. They ripen on their own schedule, driven by ethylene gas they emit themselves, and they ripen everything near them too. The good news is that this gives you a predictable system: ripen on the counter, slow it down in the fridge, and stop time in the freezer.

## How long bananas last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter (ripening) | 3–5 days | Room temperature; keep away from other fruit |
| Fridge (ripe) | 3–7 days | Skin browns; flesh stays firm |
| Freezer (peeled) | 2–3 months | Peel, halve, freeze in zip-top bags |
| Compost (overripe) | — | Mushy, fermented-smelling bananas |

## Storage conditions for bananas

Keep bananas at room temperature, off the counter and hanging if you can — hanging prevents bruising, and bruises accelerate ripening. Keep them away from apples, avocados, and tomatoes, because ethylene flows both ways: your bananas will make everything ripen faster, and everything ripening will make your bananas faster too.

The fridge is the off switch. Green bananas go in the fridge and never ripen properly, so only refrigerate bananas that are already at the ripeness you like. The cold turns the skin black in a couple of days — that's purely cosmetic. Check the flesh before assuming the banana is bad.

::tip
Wrap the stem of a bunch of bananas in plastic wrap. The stem releases the most ethylene, so wrapping it slows ripening by a day or two — an easy trick if you bought more than you can eat this week.
::

## How to tell bananas are past their prime

Brown spots mean ripe and sweet — that's when bananas are best for eating. A banana has gone too far when the flesh is mushy, the fruit smells fermented or sweet-rotten, or liquid oozes from the stem. Check the stem and the creases of the skin for mold. Brown and soft bananas are ideal for banana bread; black and oozing ones go in the bin.

## Freezing bananas

Frozen bananas are a kitchen workhorse. Peel ripe bananas, cut them in half or into chunks, and freeze them flat on a tray before transferring to bags, so they don't clump. They keep 2–3 months. Use them straight from the freezer in smoothies, or blend thawed ones into "nice cream" and baked goods. Never freeze bananas in their peel — it turns into a soggy mess.
