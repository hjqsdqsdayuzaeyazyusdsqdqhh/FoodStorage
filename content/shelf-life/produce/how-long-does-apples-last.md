Apples are the longest-lasting fruit in the produce aisle, and the difference between a crisp apple and a mealy one is almost entirely storage. Left on the counter they last a week or two; moved to the crisper they keep for over a month. The apple's main trick is that it stays good for weeks and announces precisely when it doesn't.

## How long apples last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter | 1–2 weeks | Convenient; apples turn mealy faster |
| Fridge (crisper drawer) | 4–6 weeks | Plastic bag with holes for humidity |
| Fridge (sliced) | 3–5 days | Toss in lemon juice; airtight container |
| Freezer (sliced) | Up to 8 months | Peel, core, slice; use for baking |

## Storage conditions for apples

The fridge is where apples shine. Put them in the crisper drawer — the most humid part of the refrigerator — in a perforated plastic bag that keeps moisture in while letting excess humidity escape. Cold slows the enzymes that soften apples; humidity keeps the flesh from drying and shriveling.

Keep apples away from other produce. They are heavy ethylene emitters, so a bowl of apples will ripen nearby bananas, pears, and avocados faster. And separate damaged apples from the rest: a bruised or cut apple emits far more ethylene and speeds up the whole batch.

::tip
Store apples in the crisper drawer away from potatoes and onions. Potatoes keep best in the dark and can pick up apple ethylene, and onions and apples make each other taste off. Give each its own zone.
::

## How to tell apples are past their prime

The classic signs are texture and skin. A good apple is firm, smooth, and bright. An aging apple is soft, wrinkled, or shriveled, with a dull or greasy-feeling skin. Brown, mushy patches inside are bruising — trim them and eat the rest. Fuzzy mold anywhere on the skin means discard. A whiff of fermentation or a spongy interior means the apple is done.

## Freezing apples

Frozen apples are a baker's friend. Peel, core, and slice them, toss in lemon juice or a light sugar syrup to keep them from browning, then freeze in airtight bags for up to 8 months. Use them in pies, crisps, applesauce, and smoothies. Like most high-water fruit, thawed apples lose their crunch, so plan for cooking, not snacking.
