Tomatoes are the one produce item where the best-storing choice is the worst-tasting choice. The fridge doubles their shelf life and strips their flavor. So the tomato rule is really a taste decision: keep them on the counter and eat them while they're good, or refrigerate them when you must and accept the tradeoff.

## How long tomatoes last

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter (ripening) | 3–7 days | Stem-up, away from direct sun |
| Fridge (fully ripe) | 3–5 days | Flavor and texture noticeably worse |
| Fridge (cut) | 2–3 days | Cut-side down, sealed, use soon |
| Freezer (whole/cooked) | About 2 months | For sauces and stews, not salads |

## Storage conditions for tomatoes

Ripen tomatoes on the counter, stem-up, out of direct sunlight — sunlight softens them unevenly. A paper bag with a ripe banana or apple speeds ripening if you're in a hurry, because both emit ethylene. Once a tomato is fully red and ripe, you have a choice: eat it in the next day or two, or move it to the fridge and accept mealier flesh for a few extra days.

Cut tomatoes go in the fridge, cut-side down on a plate or in a sealed container, and should be eaten within 2–3 days. Keep cut tomatoes away from other produce — they are a juicy magnet for mold spores.

::tip
For salads and caprese, keep tomatoes on the counter and pull them out of any fridge at least 30 minutes before eating. Even briefly chilled tomatoes taste better after sitting at room temperature to warm up.
::

## How to tell tomatoes are past their prime

Soft, mushy spots and wrinkled, split skin are the early warning signs. Mold — often gray-green and fuzzy, usually starting at the stem — means discard the whole tomato. A small soft spot that hasn't spread can be cut out and the rest used that day. An off, fermenting smell, or flesh that has gone watery and sour, means the tomato is done.

## Freezing tomatoes

Tomatoes freeze better than almost any other vegetable. Freeze whole ripe tomatoes in zip-top bags for up to 2 months, then thaw and peel — the skin slips right off, and they cook down beautifully into sauce, soup, and stew. For the best frozen product, cook them into sauce first. Frozen tomatoes collapse when thawed, so never plan to serve them fresh.
