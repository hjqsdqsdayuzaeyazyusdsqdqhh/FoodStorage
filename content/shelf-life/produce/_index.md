Produce is where most food waste happens, and almost all of it is a storage mistake. Berries that turn to mush in two days, lettuce that wilts in the crisper, potatoes that sprout in the pantry — each has a specific cause and a specific fix.

The master key is ethylene. Bananas, apples, tomatoes, and avocados release ethylene gas, which ripens them and ripens everything around them. That is why one brown banana turns the whole bowl brown, and why a single bad apple "spoils the bunch." Separate ethylene producers from ethylene-sensitive food — berries, greens, and most vegetables — and the sensitive food keeps twice as long.

## The counter crew

Three things prefer the counter: bananas, tomatoes, and potatoes. Tomatoes lose flavor and turn mealy in the fridge. Potatoes get sweet and brown in the fridge. Bananas finish ripening on the counter, then move to the fridge once ripe — the skin will brown, but the fruit inside stays good for days.

## The fridge crew

Everything else lasts longer cold: apples 4–6 weeks, strawberries 3–7 days, lettuce 5–7 days, mushrooms 3–7 days, onions (cut) 7–10 days. Store greens in a slightly open plastic bag or produce keeper, and keep berries dry — moisture is what rots them.

## Freezing produce

Most produce freezes for months for smoothies, soups, and baking. The exception is high-water items — lettuce, cucumbers, tomatoes — which turn to mush when thawed. Blanch vegetables before freezing to stop the enzymes that cause mush and color loss. See [how to freeze vegetables](/storage-methods/freezing/how-to-freeze-vegetables/) and [how to freeze fruit](/storage-methods/freezing/how-to-freeze-fruit/).
