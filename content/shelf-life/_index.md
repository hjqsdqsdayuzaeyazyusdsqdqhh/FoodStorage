  - q: Does food go bad faster if the fridge is warmer than 40°F?
    a: Yes. Bacteria grow much faster above 40°F. At 40–140°F — the danger zone — perishable food should not sit out for more than 2 hours (1 hour if the room is above 90°F). Keep the fridge at 40°F or below and the freezer at 0°F or below to get the storage times listed here.
  - q: Can you freeze most foods to make them last longer?
    a: "Yes. Freezing at 0°F stops bacterial growth and keeps food safe indefinitely. Quality, not safety, is what limits freezer storage: most foods are best eaten within 3–12 months. Freezing changes texture — dairy and watery produce turn soft when thawed — so freeze those for cooking, not for eating raw."
  - q: Are best-by and use-by dates expiration dates?
    a: No. Best-by dates are the manufacturer's guess at peak quality, not a food-safety cutoff. A use-by date is the last date the manufacturer recommends consuming the product at peak quality. The average household throws away hundreds of dollars a year in food that was perfectly safe — because nobody could tell how long it actually lasts. That number is the reason this site exists, and this chart is the answer.

Best-by dates are a manufacturer's guess about peak quality, not an expiration date. A carton of milk does not spoil the moment the printed date passes, and a jar of canned tomatoes does not become toxic at year three. What actually determines shelf life is storage temperature, packaging, and whether the food shows real signs of spoilage.

## The three temperatures that decide everything

Almost every shelf life on this chart comes down to three numbers:

- **Fridge: 40°F or below.** Bacteria multiply quickly above 40°F. Keep perishables here and they keep for days to weeks.
- **Freezer: 0°F or below.** Freezing stops bacterial growth entirely. Food stays safe indefinitely; quality declines over months, not days.
- **Danger zone: 40–140°F.** Never leave perishable food in this range for more than 2 hours — 1 hour if it is above 90°F outside.

Set your fridge to 37–40°F and your freezer to 0°F, and the durations in this chart apply. Let either run warmer and every number below shrinks.
