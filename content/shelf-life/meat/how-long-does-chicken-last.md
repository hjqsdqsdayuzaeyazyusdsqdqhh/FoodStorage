    a: Cooked chicken keeps 3–4 days in the refrigerator at 40°F or below. Cool it within 2 hours (1 hour if the room is above 90°F), refrigerate promptly in a sealed container, and reheat leftovers to 165°F. After 3–4 days, throw it out even if it looks fine.

Chicken is the food that makes food safety real. It is the most common source of Salmonella and Campylobacter infections, and nearly every chicken-safety rule exists because of one simple fact: you cannot see bacteria, and they do not change how chicken looks until the spoilage has already begun.

The good news is that the rules are short. Raw chicken has a 2-day fridge window. Cook it to 165°F. Do not wash it. Freeze what you will not eat in time. That covers almost everything.

## How long chicken lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge — raw whole chicken | 1–2 days | Fridge at 40°F or below |
| Fridge — raw pieces | 1–2 days | Use or freeze within 2 days |
| Fridge — cooked | 3–4 days | Refrigerate within 2 hours |
| Freezer — raw | 9–12 months (best quality) | 0°F; airtight packaging |
| Freezer — cooked | 4 months (best quality) | Cool before freezing |
| Counter | 2 hours max | Discard beyond the limit |

## Storage conditions for chicken

Raw chicken goes into the fridge the moment you get home, ideally on the bottom shelf so its juices cannot drip onto anything else. Keep it in its original packaging, and put the package on a plate or in a shallow container to catch drips. If you will not cook it within 2 days, freeze it — the 2-day rule does not wait for a busy week.

Cooked chicken gets refrigerated within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F), in shallow containers so it cools quickly. Do not leave it out overnight "to cool" — that is exactly how the bacteria in the danger zone multiply.

::safety
Raw poultry is a high-risk food. Refrigerate at 40°F or below, cook to an internal temperature of 165°F, and never thaw on the counter. Throw away any raw chicken stored in the fridge for more than 2 days, and any cooked chicken in the fridge for more than 4 days.
::

## How to tell if chicken is bad

Spoiled raw chicken is grayish or dull instead of pink, feels sticky or slimy, and gives off a sour, sulfury, "gone chicken" smell. Any of these means it goes in the bin — do not rinse it, do not cook it. Cooking kills the bacteria, but it does not remove the toxins and heat-resistant spores they can leave behind, which is why bad-smelling chicken can still make you sick.

For cooked chicken, trust the calendar more than your nose: after 3–4 days in the fridge, throw it out even if it still looks and smells fine. Leftovers do not always smell bad before they make you sick.

::warning
Do not taste raw chicken to check it, and do not rely on cooking to "save" questionable chicken. If raw chicken has been in the fridge past 2 days or smells off, discard it. No amount of cooking makes unsafe chicken safe.
::

## Freezing and thawing chicken

Chicken freezes well. Raw chicken keeps best quality 9 months for pieces and 12 months for a whole bird; cooked chicken 4 months. Freeze in airtight, moisture-proof packaging to prevent freezer burn.

Thaw chicken safely three ways: in the refrigerator (the best — plan ahead), in cold water sealed in a leak-proof bag (change the water every 30 minutes), or in the microwave (cook immediately after). Never thaw on the counter — the outside reaches the danger zone while the inside is still frozen.
