    a: Raw steak keeps 3–5 days in the fridge at 40°F or below — longer than ground beef because whole cuts have bacteria only on the surface. The exact window depends on the cut and how it was packaged. Use it or freeze it within 3–5 days, and cook it to 145°F for steaks.
  - q: How long does cooked steak last in the fridge?
    a: Cooked steak keeps 3–4 days in the refrigerator at 40°F or below. Refrigerate it within 2 hours of cooking in a sealed container. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. After 3–4 days, discard — the same 3–4 day rule applies to all cooked meat.
  - q: How long can steak be frozen?
    a: Raw steak keeps its best quality frozen for 6–12 months at 0°F and stays safe indefinitely at that temperature. Frozen meat lasts about twice as long in quality terms when vacuum-sealed. Thaw in the refrigerator, in cold water in a sealed bag, or in the microwave — never on the counter.

Steak is the meat that rewards a little patience. Because a whole cut has bacteria only on its surface — not mixed through like ground beef — it keeps for 3–5 days in the fridge, and a good dry-age or cryovac package pushes that even further. The rules are more forgiving than chicken, but they are still rules.

The one thing people get wrong with steak is confusing color with spoilage. Raw beef turns brown or gray from oxidation all the time, and it is usually fine. The real spoilage signs are texture and smell.

## How long steak lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge — raw | 3–5 days | Fridge at 40°F or below |
| Fridge — cooked | 3–4 days | Refrigerate within 2 hours |
| Freezer — raw | 6–12 months (best quality) | 0°F; airtight or vacuum-sealed |
| Freezer — cooked | 3–4 months (best quality) | Cool before freezing |
| Counter | 2 hours max | Discard beyond the limit |

## Storage conditions for steak

Keep raw steak in the fridge on a lower shelf, in its original packaging, on a plate to catch drips. The 3–5 day window assumes a fridge at 40°F or below. Store steak loosely wrapped in the fridge rather than tightly sealed — light airflow keeps the surface drier and the steak better.

If you buy steaks for a planned dinner, freeze what you will not eat within a few days. Freeze in airtight or vacuum-sealed packaging, and label with the date. Steaks freeze beautifully and lose almost nothing when thawed properly.

::safety
Beef is a high-risk food. Refrigerate at 40°F or below, cook steaks to 145°F (medium) with a 3-minute rest, and never thaw on the counter. Discard raw steak kept more than 5 days, and cooked steak kept more than 4 days.
::

## How to tell if a steak has gone bad

Check the surface. Fresh steak is red and moist, not sticky. Spoiled steak is dark gray or brown all over, sticky or slimy to the touch, and gives off a sour, funky, ammoniated smell. That smell is the strongest signal — fresh beef smells clean, almost metallic.

A dry, dark outer surface on a steak that has been refrigerated is usually oxidation, not spoilage. If the interior is still red, the smell is clean, and the surface is dry rather than sticky, trim the dark edges and cook the rest. When texture and smell both say bad, though, it goes in the bin.

::warning
Do not rely on cooking to "fix" questionable steak. If it smells sour or ammoniated, or feels slimy, discard it. Cooking kills living bacteria but does not remove the heat-stable toxins some bacteria leave behind.
::

## Freezing and thawing steak

Raw steak freezes for 6–12 months at 0°F with excellent results, especially vacuum-sealed — removing air roughly doubles the quality window and prevents freezer burn. Freeze steaks flat, individually if you want to cook one at a time.

Thaw in the refrigerator (best), in cold water in a sealed bag, or in the microwave (cook immediately). Never thaw on the counter. Cook thawed steak within 1–2 days and do not refreeze it raw.
