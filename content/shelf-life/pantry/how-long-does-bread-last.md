Bread is the food with the most backwards storage advice attached to it. People fridge their bread "to keep it fresh" and it comes out dry and leathery. The science is simple: the fridge makes bread go stale faster, and the freezer is the only place that actually stops time.

## How long bread lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter — bread box/paper bag | 3–5 days | Cool, dry, sealed from air |
| Counter — artisan, no preservatives | 2–3 days | Best eaten fresh |
| Fridge | 7–14 days but stale fast | Not recommended for most bread |
| Freezer | 3 months (best quality) | Slice first, freeze airtight |
| Counter — filled rolls/sandwiches | 1–2 days | Moist fillings breed mold |

## Storage conditions for bread

Room temperature, in a bread box or paper bag, is the right home for a loaf you'll eat this week. A bread box controls humidity — sealed enough to keep the bread from drying out, ventilated enough to keep mold away. A paper bag does most of the same job. Skip plastic bags for crusty bread: they trap moisture, soften the crust, and speed up mold.

The fridge is the stale-maker. Refrigeration accelerates starch retrogradation, the chemical process that turns soft bread firm and dry. The only breads that belong in the fridge are moist, filled ones — like deli rolls or sandwich loaf — that would otherwise grow mold within days at room temperature.

::tip
Freeze the bread you won't eat in the first week. Slice the loaf, put it in an airtight bag with the air pressed out, and freeze. Pull slices out and toast them straight from the freezer — most people can't tell it was ever frozen.
::

## How to tell bread has gone bad

Mold is the decisive sign — green, white, or black fuzzy spots anywhere on the loaf. Bread mold roots deeper than it looks, so a moldy slice means the whole loaf goes in the bin; don't just cut the spot off. A sour or musty smell is the other warning. Dry, hard bread is stale, not spoiled — revive it by toasting, steaming, or making croutons, but never revive moldy bread.

::warning
Never eat bread with visible mold, and never toast moldy bread "to kill it." Toasting does not destroy mold toxins, and the mold on one slice means spores have likely spread through the whole loaf. When in doubt, the loaf goes.
::

## Freezing bread

Bread is one of the best freezers in the kitchen. Slice the loaf before freezing so you can pull single slices, press out the air, and freeze airtight for up to 3 months — quality stays near-perfect because freezing stops staling entirely. Thaw whole loaves on the counter for a few hours, or toast slices straight from frozen. Wrap airtight to keep out freezer burn and odors.
