    a: Cooked pasta keeps 3–5 days in the refrigerator at 40°F or below. Refrigerate it within 2 hours of cooking, toss it with a little oil to stop it from sticking, and store it in a sealed container. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. After 3–5 days, discard.

Dry pasta is nearly immortal. In an airtight container it keeps for years, survives pantry chaos, and only loses quality slowly. About the only things that can kill it are the same three that threaten all dry goods: moisture, bugs, and time in a warm kitchen. Cooked pasta is a completely different story, with real fridge and freezer windows.

## How long pasta lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pantry — dry white pasta | 1–2 years | Airtight, cool, dark |
| Pantry — whole-grain pasta | 3–6 months | Oils in the grain go rancid |
| Fridge — cooked | 3–5 days | Toss in oil; sealed container |
| Freezer — cooked | About 2 months | Slightly undercooked, freeze flat |
| Freezer — baked pasta dish | 2–3 months | Assemble, freeze, bake from frozen |

## Storage conditions for pasta

Store dry pasta in airtight containers — glass jars or plastic containers — in a cool, dark pantry. The airtight seal keeps out pantry moths and moisture, both of which find their way into cardboard boxes easily. Pasta in the original box is fine short-term; transfer it if you buy in bulk.

Whole-grain and egg pastas are shorter-lived than white pasta because their ingredients (whole-grain oils, eggs) go rancid faster. Buy them in amounts you'll use within a few months, and trust your nose: whole-grain pasta that smells bitter-nutty has oxidized.

::tip
Keep pasta boxes in a drawer or container, not on an open shelf in sunlight. Light and heat are the two silent enemies of every dry staple — pasta, rice, and flour all last measurably longer in the dark and cool.
::

## How to tell pasta has gone bad

Dry pasta spoils visibly, not silently. Look for the telltales of pantry moths — webbing, tiny bugs, or stuck-together strands — and for mold, which appears as damp spots or gray fuzz and comes from moisture. A musty or rancid smell means the pasta has absorbed odor or its oils have oxidized. Any of these means discard.

Cooked pasta is simpler: sour smell, slime, or more than 3–5 days in the fridge means throw it out. Cooked pasta rarely announces spoilage before it's unpleasant, so use the calendar.

## Freezing pasta

Cooked pasta freezes well when you do it right. Cook it a minute or two underdone, drain, toss with a little oil to keep strands separate, cool completely, then freeze flat in airtight bags — it keeps about 2 months. Bake-and-freeze dishes like lasagna and baked ziti freeze for 2–3 months and can go straight from freezer to oven. Frozen pasta stays safe indefinitely; quality is the only limit.
