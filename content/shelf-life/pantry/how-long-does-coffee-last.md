Coffee is the only pantry staple that's purely about freshness, not safety. Coffee never really "goes bad" — it goes stale, and staling is the enemy of everything coffee drinkers love. Oxygen is the thief, and the whole storage game is about keeping air away from the beans.

## How long coffee lasts

| Storage method | Whole beans | Ground | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Counter — opened, airtight | 1 month | 1–2 weeks | Airtight, opaque, away from heat/light |
| Pantry — unopened bag | 3–5 months | 3–5 months | Keep sealed; store in the dark |
| Fridge | Not recommended | Not recommended | Moisture and odor pickup |
| Freezer | 3–6 months | Avoid | Whole beans only; one freeze only |

## Storage conditions for coffee

The enemies, in order: oxygen, heat, light, moisture, and odors. Store coffee in an airtight, opaque container at room temperature, away from the stove, the window, and the spice rack. Coffee is a sponge — it will happily absorb the smell of the chili powder next to it. Buy beans rather than ground when you can, and grind only what you need each morning, because grinding multiplies the surface area oxygen can attack.

::tip
Buy coffee in amounts you'll finish in two weeks, and resist bulk bags unless you have a freezer plan. Coffee is at its best in the first week after the bag opens — every week after is a measurable step down in flavor.
::

## How to tell coffee is past its prime

Coffee announces staleness by smell and taste: the aroma goes flat, and the cup tastes cardboardy, woody, or lifeless. It's not unsafe — stale coffee simply tastes bad. Real spoilage is rare and only comes from moisture, which causes visible mold or a musty smell. If you see mold, throw the coffee out and wash the container.

::note
Stale coffee is safe to drink — it just won't do its job. If your coffee tastes flat, it's stale, not spoiled. The fix is a better storage habit, not a health concern. True mold or must is the only discard trigger.
::

## Does coffee freeze well?

Freezing whole beans is a good strategy for coffee you won't drink within a few weeks. Freeze beans in airtight portions for 3–6 months, and follow three rules: only whole beans (ground coffee freezes badly), only freeze once (thawed-and-refrozen beans pick up condensation), and let the sealed container warm to room temperature before opening. Never freeze coffee in the fridge — the fridge is the worst of both worlds.
