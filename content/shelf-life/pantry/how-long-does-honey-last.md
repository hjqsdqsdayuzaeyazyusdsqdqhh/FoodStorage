Honey is the exception to every rule in this guide. While almost everything else has a shelf life measured in days, months, or years, honey has none. Sealed honey lasts indefinitely — it is one of the only foods on earth that literally does not expire.

## How long honey lasts

| Storage method | Duration | Best practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pantry (sealed jar) | Indefinite | Room temperature, away from heat and light |
| Counter (opened) | Indefinite | Keep lid tight; use a clean, dry spoon |
| Fridge | Indefinite but crystallizes | Not recommended |
| Freezer | Indefinite | May crystallize more |

## Storage conditions for honey

Honey is self-preserving — its low moisture and natural acidity stop bacteria and mold cold. The only way to "ruin" it is to invite moisture in. Keep the lid tight, store it in a dry place away from the stove, and never dip a wet spoon into the jar. A little crystallization is normal and reversible; contamination is the only real threat.

::tip
Use a clean, dry spoon every time you take honey, and never let water touch the jar's rim. Moisture is the only way honey can spoil — one wet spoon can start fermentation in a whole jar.
::

## Why honey crystallizes (and how to fix it)

Crystallization is natural, not spoilage. Over time — faster when cold, and faster for raw honey — the sugars form fine crystals and the honey turns thick and grainy. To fix it, place the sealed jar in a bowl of warm (not boiling) water for 10–15 minutes and stir. Or soften the jar in a warm spot. Never microwave honey directly, which can overheat it unevenly.

## When honey actually goes bad

Honey spoils only through contamination or moisture. Signs to look for: a sour, fermented smell or taste (fermentation), visible mold on the surface, or foaming. These happen when moisture gets in — from a wet spoon, a humid kitchen, or a jar left open. Contaminated honey is discarded. Honey that looks, smells, and tastes like honey is fine, no matter how old.

::safety
Never give honey to a baby under 1 year old. Honey can contain Clostridium botulinum spores that are harmless to older children and adults but can cause infant botulism in babies. This is the one hard rule about honey.
::

## The bottom line

Honey is the food to keep forever and never worry about. Store it sealed, keep moisture out, reliquefy it in warm water when it crystallizes, and it will stay perfect for decades. It's also a staple of any [long-term pantry](/pantry-organization/how-to-store-pantry-staples/).
